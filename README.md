# Warehouse API

Backend for a wholesale trading business — margarine, condensed milk, sunflower oil, mayonnaise,
cocoa. It answers the two questions such a business lives by: **what is in stock and what it cost**,
and **where the money is** — who owes us, who is holding cash, and what is left after expenses.

The API is JSON-LD over HTTP, generated by API Platform from Doctrine entities, with a JWT-protected
custom controller behind every operation that changes money or stock.

Money is never a float. Every amount is a `NUMERIC(18,2)` column handled through `bcmath`, and
quantities are `NUMERIC(14,3)`.

## Domain model

Two currencies, USD and UZS, run through the whole system side by side. They are **never** summed
into one number: a rate is only applied where the user explicitly supplies one.

### Documents

Every document follows the same shape — a draft is created, lines are added, then it is posted
through `POST /api/<resource>/{id}/change_status`. Posting is what writes to the ledgers; a posted
document can only be cancelled, never edited back into a draft.

- **Receipt** — goods arriving from a supplier. Posting creates a **Batch** per line, which carries
  the purchase price that later feeds FIFO.
- **Sale** — goods going out. Posting allocates each line across batches FIFO
  (`SaleItemAllocation`), writes the stock movements, computes realised profit per allocation, and
  raises the customer's debt.
- **Payment** — money coming in, in cash, by card or by transfer. It is allocated across the
  client's unsettled sales (`PaymentAllocation`), and posting reduces their debt.
- **Writeoff** — goods removed from a named batch (expiry, damage).
- **Inventory** — a stocktake. Each line is one batch with the ledger quantity snapshotted when
  the line was created and the quantity actually counted; posting books the difference as an
  `adjust` movement in whichever direction it falls. A surplus goes back onto the batch it was
  missing from, so no batch is invented and no payable to a supplier appears. The seller counts,
  the owner posts.
- **Expense** — money going out, in its own currency.

### Ledgers

Four append-only journals, all built the same way — a row is never deleted or edited; a cancellation
appends the opposite row, so the history stays evidence for both sides. Each has a denormalised
total kept in step with it:

| Journal | Denormalised total |
|---|---|
| `StockMovement` | `Product.remainingQty`, `Batch.remainingQty` |
| `Debt` | `Client.debtUsd` / `debtUzs` |
| `Profit` | aggregated on demand |
| `CashEntry` | `CashSession.balance*` / `unconfirmed*` |

### Cash float (`CashSession`)

The part that answers "where is my money". A session is a seller's period of accountability between
hand-ins — days or weeks, not a single day's till shift.

Three figures, and mixing them is a bug:

```
balance*      — what should physically be in the seller's bag
unconfirmed*  — handed to the owner, receipt not yet confirmed
debt to the company = balance + unconfirmed
```

Rules the services enforce:

- **Only cash creates an obligation.** Card and transfer go straight to the company account; they
  appear in the session's turnover but never in the balance on hand.
- **One open session per seller**, guaranteed by a partial unique index, not by a check in code.
- **A cash payment cannot be posted** unless the person accepting it has an open session. Non-cash
  can be accepted without one.
- **No advances**: an expense can only spend cash already collected, and only in that same currency.
- **Handing over is two-step** — the seller declares it (money leaves the balance immediately), the
  owner confirms it (accountability is lifted). Until then the sum sits in `unconfirmed*`.
- **Closing is the owner's job.** They enter what they actually took in; anything missing becomes an
  explicit `shortage` row rather than a silent reset to zero.

A seller only ever sees their own session and its journal — enforced in the Doctrine query
extension, so another seller's session answers `404`, not `403`.

## What is implemented

**Reference data** — categories, products (two price lists, unit codes, min-stock levels),
clients, suppliers, exchange rates.

**Stock** — receipts with per-batch cost, FIFO allocation on sale, batch-level writeoffs,
stocktaking with autofilled count sheets, full movement history, stock summary and top-sales
reports.

**Sales analysis** — turnover per day, week or month with quantity, revenue, cost of the goods
that left and the margin on them. Cost is taken from the profit journal rather than recomputed, so
the report can never disagree with a sale's own card. Note that a sale carrying lines in both
currencies is a document in both reports: the two document counts must not be added together.

**ABC analysis** — ranks the assortment by revenue, quantity or gross profit and splits it into the
classes that carry the business and the tail that only takes up shelf space. The thresholds are
shares of the total (80 / 95 by default, C being the remainder), not shares of the product count.

Both reports name a currency rather than converting: `currency` is mandatory for every money
figure, and each currency is its own ranking with its own 100 %. The ABC quantity metric is the one
exception — it is not money, so it ignores the currency and ranks the whole catalogue at once.

**Money** — payments in three methods and two currencies, manual and automatic allocation across a
client's oldest debts, debt ledger with per-client balances, debt aging, realised profit per sale
line, expenses with daily and per-currency summaries.

**Cash floats** — open/close, cash journal, two-step hand-ins, shortages on closing, per-session
summary with turnover by payment method, and an "on hand across all sellers" tile for the owner.

**Access control** — `ROLE_ADMIN` inherits `ROLE_SALES`. Sellers work with documents; only an owner
closes a float, confirms a hand-in, reads somebody else's float or count sheet, posts a stocktake,
or sees the company-wide cash tile. Sellers can list batches — they have to, to count them — but
`purchasePrice` and `rateSell` are hidden from them per property.

**Concurrency** — every posting path takes pessimistic locks in a fixed order and re-reads the
denormalised totals under the lock, so two simultaneous requests cannot spend the same money twice
or close the same session twice.

## API surface

`GET /api/docs` serves the OpenAPI/Swagger UI. Collections are paginated (20–30 items per page) and
filterable by date range, search and ordering.

**Resources:** `categories`, `products`, `suppliers`, `clients`, `exchange_rates`, `receipts`,
`receipt_items`, `batches`, `sales`, `sale_items`, `sale_item_allocations`, `writeoffs`,
`writeoff_items`, `inventories`, `inventory_items`, `stock_movements`, `payments`,
`payment_allocations`, `debts`, `profits`,
`expenses`, `cash_sessions`, `cash_entries`, `users`.

**Operations beyond CRUD:**

```

POST /api/cash_sessions/{id}/handover    declare a hand-in
```

Report endpoints are `POST` by design (they take a filter body) and answer `201`.

## Running it

Everything runs in Docker. `./` is mounted into the containers, so code changes need no rebuild.

**1. Configure.** `.env` is tracked and holds the defaults. Override anything machine-specific in an
untracked `.env.local` — at least `DOCKER_PROJECT_NAME`, `DOCKER_NGINX_PORT`,
`DOCKER_DATABASE_PORT`, `DATABASE_PASSWORD`, `APP_SECRET` and `JWT_PASSPHRASE`.

`DATABASE_URL` is derived from `DOCKER_PROJECT_NAME` and `DATABASE_PASSWORD`; in the `test`
environment Doctrine appends a `_test` suffix to the database name automatically.

**2. Start the stack.**

```bash
docker compose up -d --build
```

Three services come up: `php` (fpm), `nginx` (published on `127.0.0.1:${DOCKER_NGINX_PORT}`) and
`db` (PostgreSQL on `127.0.0.1:${DOCKER_DATABASE_PORT}`). Two optional profiles exist and are off by
default: `--profile backup` (nightly dump to Telegram) and `--profile mailserver`.

**3. Install.**

```bash
docker compose exec php composer install
docker compose exec php bin/console ask:install
```

`ask:install` waits for the database, runs `ask:deploy` (migrations, cache, permissions) and
generates the JWT keypair into `config/jwt/`.

**4. Create the first administrator.** The API cannot create users without an existing admin, so the
first one is made from the CLI:

```bash
docker compose exec php bin/console ask:users:create \
  --email=owner@example.com --role=ROLE_ADMIN \
  --first-name=Owner --last-name=Ownerov --password=secret
```

Run it without options for an interactive prompt with a generated password.

The API is now at `http://127.0.0.1:${DOCKER_NGINX_PORT}/api`, docs at `/api/docs`.

### Authentication

```bash
curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8507/api/users/auth \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/ld+json' \
  -d '{"email":"owner@example.com","password":"secret"}'
```

Returns `accessToken` (1 day) and `refreshToken` (2 months); send the access token as
`Authorization: Bearer <token>`. Lifetimes are set by `TOKEN_ACCESS_EXPIRATION_PERIOD` and
`TOKEN_REFRESH_EXPIRATION_PERIOD`.

## Demo data

`ask:demo:seed` fills the database with a realistic trading year. It goes through the same factories
and services as the API — draft, lines, post — so batches, FIFO allocations and every ledger come out
exactly as real usage would produce them, including the cash floats.

```bash
docker compose exec php bin/console ask:demo:seed \
  --categories=20 --products=260 --clients=140 --suppliers=60 \
  --receipts=320 --sales=1400 --writeoffs=140 \
  --sellers=5 --sessions=9 --expenses=220 --months=12
```

It creates the demo sellers itself (`sales1@example.com` … `sales5@example.com`, password from
`--seller-password`, default `string`) and reuses them on a rerun. An administrator must already
exist.

Each seller's year is cut into consecutive float periods, and every period is replayed in the only
order the services accept — open, collect, spend, hand over, close — leaving the last one open with
an unconfirmed hand-in. Timestamps are rewritten at the end: every factory stamps "now", so without
that pass a year of documents would carry today's date in every ledger.

Expect roughly an hour for the volumes above; the identity map is never cleared, so the sales phase
slows down as it goes.

## Tests

The suite runs against a real `<project>_test` database with real migrations, and drives everything
through HTTP — no service is called directly except where a race has to be simulated.

```bash
make test-api          # creates/migrates the test DB, loads fixtures, runs both suites
make test              # phpunit only, against whatever state the test DB is in
make reset-test-db     # drop, recreate, migrate, reload fixtures
```

- `tests/ApiTest/` — ~370 tests over the whole API: happy paths, guards, roles, validation.
- `tests/Integration/` — races that HTTP cannot reproduce, because the kernel reboots between
  requests. A "concurrent request" is simulated with raw SQL against an entity already in the
  identity map — the exact state a second worker is in after waiting out a `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`.

Two conventions worth knowing before writing a test:

- Look entities up by business key with `findIriBy`, never by id — fixtures purge with `DELETE` and
  Postgres sequences keep climbing.
- `assertResponseStatusCodeSame()` inspects the **most recently created** client, not the one you
  called `request()` on. Cash tests, which always have a seller and an owner, use
  `assertStatus($code, $client)` from `CashTestCase` instead.

## Layout

```
src/
  Entity/           Doctrine entities; API Platform resources are declared here as attributes
  Controller/       one invokable controller per non-CRUD operation
  Service/          business operations: posting, cancelling, allocating, closing a float
  Component/        domain building blocks — factories, DTOs, enums, typed exceptions
  Repository/       queries, including the locking helpers used by the posting paths
  Command/          the ask:* CLI
  DataFixtures/     the fixed dataset the test suite runs against
migrations/         schema history
tests/              ApiTest (HTTP) and Integration (races)
docker/             php, nginx, postgres, backup and mailserver images
```

Business rules live in `src/Service`, never in entity constraints: API Platform validates on
`kernel.view`, which is **after** a custom controller has already run, so an `Assert` on an entity
cannot protect an invariant. Guards belong in the service that performs the operation.

## Conventions

- Comments and identifiers are in English. Messages addressed to the user — exception texts,
  validation messages, document notes — are in Russian, because that is what the sellers read.
- Amounts are strings end to end, compared and combined with `bccomp`/`bcadd`/`bcmul`. A float in a
  money path is a bug.
- Cancelling never deletes a ledger row; it appends the opposite one.
- Pessimistic locks are taken in a fixed order, and any denormalised total is re-read **under** the
  lock (`refresh($e, LockMode::PESSIMISTIC_WRITE)`), never trusted from the in-memory copy.
